How an image becomes a palette

Five steps, all in your browser. Every figure is live, so press, drag, and adjust as you read.

Step 1 · Sample

Shrink it, then read every pixel

The image is scaled down until its longest side is at most 120 px. Pixels that are more than half transparent are skipped.

Each remaining pixel is three numbers from 0 to 255: red, green and blue. Use those as coordinates and every pixel becomes a point inside a cube.

Step 2 · Measure

Similar colours are close points

To compare two colours, measure the straight line between their points:

d = √(ΔR² + ΔG² + ΔB²)

The three coloured legs in the figure are ΔR, ΔG and ΔB. The dashed line is the distance. It runs from 0 (identical) to about 441 (black to white).

Step 3 · Cluster

k-means finds the centres of the crowds

To get a palette of k colours, the studio looks for k centres, each sitting in the middle of a crowd of pixels:

  1. Seed Start at a random pixel. Each next centre is the pixel farthest from the centres so far.
  2. Assign Every pixel joins its nearest centre.
  3. Update Each centre moves to the average colour of its pixels.
  4. Repeat Assign and update again until the centres stop moving, at most 12 rounds.

The centres where it settles are the palette.

Step 4 · Compare

Try three times, keep the tightest

The first centre is random, so two runs can settle on different palettes. Each run is scored by its error: the sum of the squared distances from every pixel to its centre.

Lower error means the centres fit the pixels more closely. The studio runs k-means three times and keeps the lowest.

Step 5 · Tidy

Merge twins, then sort by light

Centres closer than 28 apart are merged, so you never get two near-identical swatches. The rest are sorted dark to light by luminance:

L = 0.299 R + 0.587 G + 0.114 B

R 30% G 59% B 11%

Green counts most because our eyes are most sensitive to it. Finally the swatches are drawn: click one to copy its hex code, or save the palette as a PNG.
